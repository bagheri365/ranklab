# M2.4 — Paper-Style Results and Discussion

## Results

### Primary model selection is stable

Across all four primary evaluation cells, Popularity is the decisive winner
under the frozen M0.20 simultaneous inference rule. The primary cells combine
two logging regimes (standard and randomized) with two behavioral targets
(`is_click` and `long_view`).

Macro NDCG@10 is:

| Logging regime | Target | Popularity | BPR | LightGCN |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| standard | `is_click` | **0.716524** | 0.676985 | 0.676862 |
| standard | `long_view` | **0.648404** | 0.601746 | 0.601590 |
| randomized | `is_click` | **0.432459** | 0.343522 | 0.343527 |
| randomized | `long_view` | **0.370164** | 0.265048 | 0.265314 |

Thus, the identity of the top-ranked recommender is stable across the two
logging regimes and two target definitions examined here.

BPR and LightGCN are not decisively separated in any of the four primary
cells. Their descriptive ordering changes in small ways across cells, but the
frozen simultaneous intervals for BPR-minus-LightGCN include zero throughout.
Accordingly, RankLab does not interpret those small ordering changes as
evidence that one learned model is superior to the other.

### Comparative margins are not stable across logging regimes

Although winner identity is stable, the size of Popularity's advantage is not.

For `is_click`, the Popularity-minus-BPR margin is approximately 0.0395 under
standard logging and 0.0889 under randomized exposure. For `long_view`, the
corresponding margins are approximately 0.0467 and 0.1051.

The frozen cross-regime contrast

`G_AB = D_AB(randomized) - D_AB(standard)`

is therefore positive for comparisons of Popularity against the learned
models. For Popularity minus BPR, the native-user `G_AB` point estimate is
approximately +0.0494 for `is_click` and +0.0585 for `long_view`. The matched-
user sensitivity produces the same qualitative pattern.

This means that an offline comparison can preserve the same winning model while
materially changing the apparent size of the winner's advantage.

### Target definition also changes comparative margins

The behavioral target changes the magnitude of model separation. Under
randomized exposure, moving from `is_click` to `long_view` increases the
Popularity-minus-BPR margin by approximately +0.0162 in the native analysis.
The corresponding matched-user contrast is smaller, approximately +0.0050.

The difference between native and matched-user estimates indicates that some of
the target contrast is associated with target-specific eligible-user
composition. A smaller positive within-common-user contrast remains.

Under standard logging, the same target contrast is positive but smaller.

### Support sensitivities preserve the qualitative result

The pre-specified shared-tabs sensitivity preserves the primary qualitative
pattern. Popularity remains descriptively first in all four sensitivity cells,
while BPR-LightGCN 95% within-cell intervals cross zero throughout.

The descriptive `tab=1` sensitivity also preserves this pattern. RankLab does
not assign a semantic UI meaning to numeric tab ID 1 because the public
KuaiRand documentation does not map every numeric tab ID to a semantic UI
label.

Under both support sensitivities, the Popularity advantage remains markedly
larger under randomized exposure than under standard logging. This makes the
main margin-instability result robust to the two frozen support restrictions.

## Discussion

### Winner identity and effect-size stability are different questions

The central result is:

> **Winner identity is stable, but comparative margins are not.**

This distinction matters for offline recommender evaluation. A practitioner
using only the identity of the top-ranked model would reach the same selection
decision in all four primary cells. A practitioner using the magnitude of
offline model differences, however, would obtain substantially different
answers depending on the logged exposure process and behavioral target.

RankLab therefore separates two notions of robustness:

1. **selection robustness** — whether the same model is chosen;
2. **margin robustness** — whether the estimated pairwise advantage is stable.

The benchmark shows the first but not the second.

### Randomized exposure changes the apparent strength of the winner

The largest systematic shift occurs across logging regimes. Popularity's
advantage over BPR and LightGCN is much larger in the randomized-exposure log
than in the standard-policy log.

This should not be interpreted as a causal effect of randomized logging.
RankLab compares offline evaluation outcomes constructed from two different
logged exposure processes. The exposure processes can change which items are
observed, candidate-set composition, user eligibility, and the relationship
between relevance labels and available alternatives.

The result is therefore about the sensitivity of offline comparative
measurement to the logging regime, not about what would happen if one logging
policy causally replaced another.

### Target choice is also part of the evaluation design

`is_click` and `long_view` are strongly related but not interchangeable.
RankLab intentionally evaluates the same trained checkpoints against both
targets instead of tuning separately for each one.

The larger Popularity margin under `long_view`, especially in randomized
exposure, shows that behavioral target choice can change comparative effect
sizes even when top-model identity remains unchanged.

The native-versus-matched contrast further shows why denominator composition
should be reported rather than hidden. Changes in which users have valid
target-specific rankings contribute to the observed target contrast.

### Learned-model differences are unresolved

BPR and LightGCN remain very close under the frozen evaluation protocol. Their
small descriptive rank reversals across regime or target are not accompanied by
decisive pairwise separation.

RankLab therefore does not claim that the graph-based model is better than the
matrix-factorization model, or vice versa, on this benchmark.

This is also a reminder that a more complex recommender need not produce a
meaningfully different offline ranking result under a particular logged
candidate construction.

### Sensitivity analysis supports, but does not replace, the primary analysis

The shared-tabs sensitivity was pre-specified as a support robustness check.
The `tab=1` analysis is descriptive only. Neither sensitivity expands the
frozen primary multiplicity family.

Both checks preserve the same qualitative conclusion, which strengthens the
interpretation that the primary result is not driven solely by the broadest
shared-user/shared-video support definition.

### Limitations

Several limitations bound the interpretation.

First, the logging-regime comparison is observational at the level of the
offline benchmark and is not a causal treatment-effect design.

Second, Popularity is deterministic, while BPR and LightGCN are summarized over
five frozen seeds. The inference procedure accounts for this by averaging the
stochastic seeds inside user before bootstrap resampling.

Third, the two learned models are not decisively separated under the frozen
primary simultaneous intervals. Small descriptive differences should not be
overinterpreted.

Fourth, `tab=1` remains descriptive because the public KuaiRand documentation
does not provide a semantic label for every numeric tab code.

Fifth, target-specific eligibility composition matters, as shown by the gap
between native and matched-user target contrasts.

Finally, model-selection tuning was not perfectly symmetric: BPR searched a
regularization grid, whereas LightGCN used a fixed regularization value while
searching architecture and learning-rate choices.
