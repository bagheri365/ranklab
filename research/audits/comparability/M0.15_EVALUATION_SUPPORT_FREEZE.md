# M0.15 — Evaluation Support and Scenario Comparability Freeze

## Status

**Primary evaluation entity support: FROZEN**

**Primary tab restriction: NONE**

**Shared-tab sensitivity: FROZEN**

**tab=1 descriptive sensitivity: FROZEN**

**Candidate collapse/relevance semantics: NOT YET FROZEN**

**M1 evaluation: STILL BLOCKED**

**Overall M0 protocol: UNFROZEN**

This milestone freezes which evaluation rows are eligible for the primary
standard-policy versus randomized-policy comparison.

It does not yet freeze how repeated user-video exposures are collapsed into a
ranking candidate or how target relevance is assigned. Those are separate
evaluation decisions.

---

## 1. Why native support is not primary

The standard and randomized evaluation logs do not contain exactly the same
users and items.

The support audit found:

- shared users: 25,877;
- shared videos: 6,618;
- shared tabs: `{1,2,11,14}`.

The randomized log contains additional users/items not present in the standard
evaluation log.

Comparing completely native entity populations would mix two changes:

1. exposure policy;
2. evaluation entity population.

RankLab therefore aligns the user and item universes before the primary regime
comparison.

---

## 2. Frozen primary support

Primary evaluation support is:

> rows whose user belongs to the intersection of evaluation users across the
> standard and randomized logs, and whose video belongs to the intersection of
> evaluation videos across those logs.

Equivalently:

`user ∈ U_standard ∩ U_randomized`

and

`video ∈ I_standard ∩ I_randomized`.

No tab restriction is applied in the primary analysis.

---

## 3. Shared entities, not shared exposed pairs

RankLab does **not** require an exposed `(user_id, video_id)` pair to appear in
both regimes.

That is deliberate.

The scientific variable of interest is the exposure/logging regime. Requiring
pair-level intersection would discard regime-specific exposures and move the
analysis toward a different estimand in which much of the exposure-policy
difference had already been removed.

The primary restriction therefore aligns:

- users;
- items;

while preserving each regime's own logged exposure pairs.

---

## 4. Observed retention under the primary restriction

From the frozen support audit:

### Standard-policy evaluation

The shared-user/shared-item restriction retains all 295,497 rows.

This is expected because the standard evaluation users/items are already
contained within the corresponding randomized entity universe for the rows
observed in this dataset.

### Randomized-policy evaluation

The shared-user/shared-item restriction retains:

- 1,011,930 rows;
- 85.3187% of randomized evaluation rows;
- 25,877 users;
- 6,618 videos.

This is a substantial retained randomized sample while aligning the entity
universe used by the two regimes.

Retention must be reported in final results.

---

## 5. Why no tab restriction is primary

The shared tab set is:

`{1,2,11,14}`.

However, the public KuaiRand documentation identifies `tab` as a scenario ID
without providing a reliable semantic mapping for each numeric value.

Therefore RankLab does not make an unexplained numeric scenario restriction the
primary estimand.

The primary analysis keeps all tabs after shared-user/shared-item filtering.

This maximizes retained exposure variation without inventing semantic labels.

---

## 6. Shared-tab sensitivity analysis

A predeclared sensitivity analysis additionally restricts both regimes to:

`tab ∈ {1,2,11,14}`

after applying shared-user/shared-item support.

The support audit observed for standard evaluation:

- 232,914 rows;
- 78.8211% row retention relative to native standard evaluation.

This sensitivity asks whether conclusions persist when analysis is limited to
scenario IDs observed in both evaluation regimes.

It is not promoted to the primary estimand.

---

## 7. tab=1 descriptive sensitivity

A second predeclared sensitivity additionally restricts both regimes to:

`tab = 1`

after applying shared-user/shared-item support.

Observed audit retention:

### Standard

- 223,513 rows;
- 75.6397% of native standard rows;
- 24,537 users;
- 5,830 videos.

### Randomized

- 1,005,226 rows;
- 84.7535% of native randomized rows;
- 25,611 users;
- 6,614 videos.

The public semantics of numeric tab ID 1 are not treated as known.

Therefore this is reported only as a descriptive scenario slice and robustness
analysis.

---

## 8. Target prevalence does not justify further matching

The earlier support audit showed that the large standard-versus-randomized
difference in `is_click` and `long_view` prevalence remains after the
shared-entity and tab restrictions.

Therefore RankLab does not continue matching until target prevalence becomes
similar.

Doing so would condition the evaluation population on properties closely tied
to the behavioral outcomes and would risk replacing the exposure-regime
question with a post-treatment matching exercise.

The remaining prevalence difference is part of the phenomenon being studied,
not a defect to be mechanically erased.

---

## 9. Exposure depth remains regime-specific

Randomized exposure has much greater exposure depth per user than standard
exposure.

The primary support rule does not attempt to equalize the number of logged
exposures per user.

That would again remove part of the logging-policy difference.

Instead:

- the ranking unit and aggregation will be user-level;
- retention and candidate-depth distributions must be reported;
- uncertainty procedures will later use user clustering.

The exact evaluation candidate and statistical rules remain separate M0
decisions.

---

## 10. Frozen support hierarchy

### Primary

`shared_users_and_videos`

- shared users;
- shared videos;
- no tab restriction;
- retain regime-native exposed pairs.

### Sensitivity A

`shared_users_videos_and_tabs`

- primary shared entity support;
- `tab ∈ {1,2,11,14}`.

### Sensitivity B

`shared_users_videos_tab_1`

- primary shared entity support;
- `tab = 1`;
- descriptive numeric scenario slice only.

### Descriptive-only native view

Native standard and randomized support may be reported descriptively for
retention/context, but native-support model comparisons are not the primary
RankLab result.

---

## 11. What M0.15 freezes

Frozen:

- primary support = shared users + shared videos;
- no primary tab restriction;
- preservation of regime-specific exposed user-item pairs;
- shared-tabs sensitivity;
- tab=1 descriptive sensitivity;
- retention reporting requirement.

---

## 12. What remains open

Not frozen here:

- duplicate user-video row collapse in evaluation;
- target-specific relevance collapse;
- minimum candidate-set size;
- `< K` behavior;
- zero-relevance-user handling;
- unseen trained-entity behavior;
- exact primary metric fields in the protocol registry;
- seed aggregation;
- model-ordering rule;
- bootstrap and confidence intervals.

Those choices must be frozen before M1 metrics are exposed.

---

## 13. Decision

RankLab's primary standard-versus-randomized comparison uses the intersection
of evaluation users and the intersection of evaluation items, while preserving
the distinct exposure pairs generated by each logging regime.

No numeric `tab` restriction is primary.

Shared-tab and `tab=1` restrictions are predeclared sensitivity analyses.
