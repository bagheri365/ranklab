# M0.14 — Popularity Baseline and Ranking Tie-Break Freeze

## Status

**Popularity score definition: FROZEN**

**Ranking tie-break rule: FROZEN**

**Popularity hyperparameter search: NOT APPLICABLE**

**Popularity stochastic seed set: NOT APPLICABLE**

**M1 evaluation: STILL BLOCKED**

**Overall M0 protocol: UNFROZEN**

This milestone closes the remaining model-side ambiguity for the deterministic Popularity baseline.

## 1. Popularity score definition

The existing `PopularityRanker` is retained without changing its scientific semantics.

For each video, its score is the number of distinct users with a positive clicked user-video pair in the frozen Apr 9–16 standard-policy training slice.

Training interactions use the same pair-collapsed any-click semantics as the frozen training contract. Repeated clicks by the same user on the same video do not increase popularity more than once.

The ranker is deliberately non-personalized. An item not observed as a positive training item receives score `0`.

## 2. Why a ranking rule was required

Popularity produces many exact score ties, including videos clicked by the same number of users and candidate videos with zero positive training users.

Before M0.14, RankLab had no project-level rule specifying how equal model scores must be ordered. An evaluator could therefore inherit CSV row order, candidate construction order, dictionary insertion order, or implementation-specific sort behavior.

That would make metrics depend on incidental ordering rather than model scores.

## 3. Frozen deterministic rank order

For every model and evaluation candidate set, RankLab ranks by:

1. **model score descending**;
2. **`video_id` ascending** on an exact score tie.

The rule applies uniformly to Popularity, BPR, and LightGCN.

## 4. Why `video_id` ascending

The secondary key must be deterministic, label-independent, exposure-row-order-independent, available in both regimes, and identical across models.

Ascending `video_id` satisfies those requirements. It is not claimed to be behaviorally meaningful; it exists only to resolve an otherwise undefined exact score tie reproducibly.

## 5. Candidate uniqueness

The ranking utility requires unique candidate item IDs within each user-level ranking unit.

This matches the frozen validation convention of collapsing logged user-video candidate pairs. The final evaluation candidate-construction protocol must preserve this uniqueness property.

## 6. Popularity fitting status

Popularity requires no hyperparameter search and no stochastic primary seed fitting.

Its fitted state is fully determined by the frozen Apr 9–16 training data, pair-collapsed any-click semantics, and distinct-positive-user counting per item.

Serialization of the deterministic score table, if used later, is an engineering detail rather than a tuning decision.

## 7. Leakage guardrail

Popularity scoring and tie-breaking use neither validation labels nor either evaluation regime's labels, and they do not use `long_view`.

## 8. What M0.14 freezes

Frozen:

- Popularity family: clicked-pair popularity;
- score: distinct positive users per video;
- training window: Apr 9–16;
- unknown or non-positive-only item score: zero;
- non-personalized scoring;
- global deterministic ordering: score descending, then `video_id` ascending;
- unique candidate item requirement.

## 9. What remains open

Still open before M1:

- primary standard/randomized support definition;
- scenario/tab restriction policy;
- final candidate construction;
- exact K and zero-relevance evaluation edge cases;
- seed aggregation and model-ordering rules;
- statistical inference and bootstrap procedure;
- final overall M0 protocol freeze.

## 10. Decision

The existing Popularity scoring semantics are accepted as the frozen deterministic baseline.

All model evaluation must use descending model score followed by ascending `video_id` on exact ties.

M1 must not use incidental candidate input order to resolve score ties.
