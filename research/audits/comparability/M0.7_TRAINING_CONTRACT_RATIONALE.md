# M0.7 — Training Contract Rationale and Decision Record

## Status

**Training subsection: FROZEN**

**Overall M0 protocol: UNFROZEN**

This note records how RankLab arrived at its frozen **primary training contract** using only pre-test evidence from the standard-policy history. It does **not** freeze the remaining M0 decisions for primary evaluation, matched-support analysis, uncertainty estimation, or final M1 model-selection rules.

No Apr 22–May 8 standard-policy or randomized-exposure evaluation labels were used to select the training interaction, temporal validation window, or negative-sampling rule.

---

## 1. Purpose

RankLab studies whether the **same trained recommender systems** lead to stable offline model-selection decisions when evaluated under different logging regimes and behavioral targets.

To make that comparison interpretable, the training setup had to be fixed before M1 and satisfy several requirements:

1. use **standard-policy history only** for primary model training;
2. avoid leakage from the Apr 22–May 8 primary evaluation period;
3. provide enough behavioral support for BPR and LightGCN;
4. define positives and negatives consistently at the user–video pair level;
5. retain enough users for pairwise training;
6. make validation deep enough to support ranking-based hyperparameter selection;
7. keep the same fitted checkpoints fixed across all later regime × target evaluations;
8. revise pre-study preferences when the observed M0 evidence did not support them.

The final contract below was therefore **evidence-driven**, not chosen because it was the first design proposed in the project plan.

---

# 2. Original design preference

The original plan preferred a primary training interaction that was **distinct from both evaluation targets**:

- `is_click`
- `long_view`

The motivation was clean separation:

> train each recommender once using one frozen interaction definition, then evaluate those fixed checkpoints under two different behavioral targets.

This was attractive because neither primary evaluation target would also serve as the training signal.

However, RankLab treated this as a **preference to test during M0**, not as a rule that had to survive regardless of the data.

---

# 3. Evidence reviewed before freezing

The decision used only the standard-policy history file:

```text
log_standard_4_08_to_4_21_pure.csv
```

Observed dates in that file were:

```text
2022-04-09 through 2022-04-21
```

The file contained:

- **1,141,112 rows**
- **26,210 unique users**
- **7,538 unique videos**

The Apr 22–May 8 standard-policy and randomized-exposure files remained outside this training-contract decision.

---

# 4. Candidate training interaction A — social actions only

The first target-independent behavioral candidate was:

```text
is_like
OR is_follow
OR is_comment
OR is_forward
```

Across the full standard-policy history:

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---:|
| Positive prevalence | 2.225% |
| Positive rows | 25,390 |
| Unique positive videos | 3,861 |
| Users with ≥1 positive | 7,419 |
| Users with ≥2 positives | 3,777 |
| Users with ≥5 positives | 1,308 |

This signal was behaviorally meaningful and remained distinct from both `is_click` and `long_view`.

However, its support was weak for a primary collaborative-training contract.

Under the initial provisional three-day validation slice, Apr 19–21:

| Validation statistic | Value |
|---|---:|
| Positive rows | 1,306 |
| Unique positive videos | 810 |
| Users with ≥1 positive | 974 |
| Users with ≥2 positives | 213 |
| Users with ≥5 positives | 14 |

## Decision

**Rejected as the primary training interaction.**

## Reason

The signal was too sparse for robust BPR/LightGCN fitting and especially too sparse for reliable hyperparameter selection in the validation period.

The rejection was based on support and feasibility, not on downstream M1 performance.

# 5. Candidate training interaction B — downstream-engagement composite

RankLab next tested a broader behavioral composite:

```text
is_profile_enter
OR is_like
OR is_follow
OR is_comment
OR is_forward
```

The purpose was to increase behavioral coverage while still avoiding direct use of `is_click` or `long_view`.

Across the full standard-policy history:

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---:|
| Positive prevalence | 4.546% |
| Positive rows | 51,871 |
| Unique positive videos | 4,841 |
| Users with ≥1 positive | 13,361 |
| Users with ≥2 positives | 8,711 |
| Users with ≥5 positives | 3,303 |

This was materially denser than social-only engagement.

Under the provisional Apr 19–21 validation slice:

| Validation statistic | Value |
|---|---:|
| Positive prevalence | 3.895% |
| Positive rows | 2,388 |
| Unique positive videos | 1,234 |
| Users with ≥1 positive | 1,874 |
| Users with ≥2 positives | 348 |
| Users with ≥5 positives | 19 |

## Decision

**Rejected as the primary training interaction.**

## Reason

The composite improved coverage but remained too sparse in the validation period for a stable primary collaborative-training and tuning protocol.

At this point, continuing to invent increasingly complicated behavioral composites solely to preserve training-target independence would have introduced more arbitrary modeling choices than it removed.

---

# 6. Candidate training interaction C — all logged exposures

A maximally dense alternative was:

```text
every standard-policy logged exposure = positive interaction
```

Across the full standard-history file this yields:

- **1,141,112 positive rows**
- **26,210 users with at least one interaction**
- **7,538 positive videos**

Technically, this provides excellent coverage.

Conceptually, however, it changes the learned object.

A recommender trained with every exposure treated as positive would primarily learn the structure of what the historical policy **showed**, rather than a user-response preference signal.

## Decision

**Rejected as the preferred primary personalized training interaction.**

## Reason

The density is excellent, but the semantic interpretation is weak for RankLab's primary recommender comparison.

This distinction matters because RankLab already studies differences between logging regimes. Making historical exposure itself the primary positive preference signal would entangle the personalized models even more strongly with the standard logging policy.

---

# 7. Revision of the original training-target independence preference

M0 therefore established three facts:

1. social-only behavior was too sparse;
2. the broader downstream-engagement composite was still too sparse;
3. all-exposure training was dense but semantically unattractive as a preference signal.

Keeping the original requirement that training supervision be distinct from both primary evaluation targets would have required one of the following:

- accepting an under-supported behavioral label;
- constructing a more arbitrary composite;
- or training primarily on historical exposure structure.

Those alternatives were less defensible than revising the original preference.

## Decision

**Relax the requirement that the primary training interaction be distinct from both evaluation targets.**

This change was made during M0, before M1 comparative evaluation.

That is an intentional example of the RankLab principle:

> pre-test evidence may revise the protocol; post-test attractiveness may not.

---

# 8. Why `is_click` was selected for primary training

RankLab selected `is_click` as the primary implicit-feedback training signal.

The standard-policy history had an overall click prevalence of approximately **46.3%**, giving far stronger support than the target-independent behavioral alternatives.

The choice was preferred because:

1. `is_click` is a direct observed user-response signal rather than exposure alone;
2. it is dense enough for BPR and LightGCN;
3. it supports broad user and item coverage;
4. it allows `long_view` to remain a deeper downstream behavioral evaluation target;
5. all primary models can still be trained once and evaluated with the exact same checkpoints across both targets;
6. no target-specific retraining is required.

This changes the interpretation of the two evaluation targets:

### Target A — `is_click`

A **training-aligned** evaluation target.

### Target B — `long_view`

A **downstream behavioral** evaluation target not used for primary model training or hyperparameter selection.

The resulting target-stability question is therefore:

> Does the recommender selected under the training-aligned click objective remain preferred when the same checkpoints are evaluated using the deeper `long_view` behavior?

This interpretation must remain explicit in RankLab reporting.

# 9. Temporal split selection

## 9.1 Initial provisional split

The first leakage-safe candidate used:

```text
Training:   Apr 9–18
Validation: Apr 19–21
```

This preserved Apr 22–May 8 completely for later primary evaluation.

Training-to-validation overlap was already excellent:

- validation user coverage from training: approximately **98.6%**
- validation video coverage from training: approximately **99.8%**

However, the validation ranking sets were shallow:

| 3-day validation statistic | Value |
|---|---:|
| Median candidate pairs/user | 2 |
| 90th percentile | 7 |
| Users with ≥10 candidates | 897 |
| Users with positive and ≥2 candidates | 9,595 |

NDCG@10 is still mathematically defined when a user has fewer than 10 candidates under RankLab's frozen `<K` semantics, but a validation set dominated by 2–7 candidate rankings would make `@10` a weak description of the actual tuning problem.

# 10. User–video pair construction

Training operates on **collapsed user–video pairs**, not raw exposure rows.

For each user–video pair in the frozen training slice:

## Positive pair

A pair is positive if **any** standard-policy training exposure for that pair has:

```text
is_click = 1
```

## Eligible logged negative

A pair is eligible as a negative only if:

1. the video was actually exposed to that user in the frozen training slice; and
2. that user–video pair has **no** `is_click=1` exposure anywhere in the training slice.

This guarantees that the same collapsed user–video pair cannot simultaneously appear as positive and negative.

The intended pairwise relation is:

```text
clicked exposed item
>
non-clicked exposed item
```

rather than:

```text
clicked item
>
arbitrary unseen catalog item
```

This was preferred because an exposed non-clicked item supplies stronger negative evidence than an item that may simply never have been shown.

The negative still must not be interpreted as a verified dislike.

---

# 11. Why strict distinct-negative matching was rejected

The first BPR-style proposal implicitly required enough distinct negative pairs to assign a different negative to every positive.

Under the initial three-day validation candidate split, training contained:

- **25,162 users with positive pairs**
- **24,039 users with both positive and eligible negative pairs**
- only **11,886 users** feasible for a strict distinct 1:1 negative assignment

The problem was therefore not absence of negative evidence for most users.

The problem was requiring:

```text
number of unique negatives >= number of positives
```

BPR-style pairwise learning does not require a unique negative for every positive pair.

## Decision

Reject strict distinct-negative matching.

---

# 12. Frozen negative-sampling rule

For BPR-style training, including the pairwise objective used with LightGCN:

```text
1 eligible negative per positive pair per epoch
same-user negative pool
uniform sampling
with replacement
resampled every epoch
deterministic given the model training seed
```

A user is eligible for pairwise BPR/LightGCN training when the frozen training slice contains:

```text
>= 1 positive pair
AND
>= 1 eligible same-user logged negative pair
```

Users with positive pairs but no eligible logged negative are excluded from pairwise training and must be counted and reported.

Under the final five-day validation split, the frozen training slice contains:

| Training statistic | Value |
|---|---:|
| Positive pairs | 463,260 |
| Negative-only pairs | 504,572 |
| Users with positive pairs | 24,844 |
| Users with both positive and negative pairs | 23,638 |
| Users excluded for zero logged negatives | 1,206 |
| Positive-user retention | 95.15% |

## Reason

Sampling with replacement retains almost all users who have meaningful pairwise evidence while avoiding arbitrary unseen-item negatives and avoiding the unnecessary requirement for distinct negatives.

---

# 13. LightGCN graph construction

For LightGCN, the user–item graph is built from **positive click pairs only**.

Eligible logged-negative pairs:

- are used by the BPR-style training loss;
- are not inserted as positive graph edges.

This keeps the graph semantics aligned with the frozen primary interaction definition.

---

# 14. Frozen validation objective

## Ranking unit

One validation user.

## Candidate set

That user's unique logged user–video pairs in the frozen validation period:

```text
Apr 17–21
```

## Relevance

Binary pair-level relevance:

```text
relevant = any is_click=1 for that user-video pair in validation
```

## Primary validation metric

```text
macro-average NDCG@10
```

over eligible validation users.

RankLab's exact NDCG edge-case semantics, deterministic tie behavior, and treatment of candidate sets shorter than `K` remain governed by the M0 evaluation/metric contract.

### Important limitation

Even under the selected five-day validation window:

- median candidates/user = **4**
- P90 candidates/user = **12**

Therefore many validation users have fewer than 10 candidates.

NDCG@10 remains usable under the predeclared `<K` behavior, but reporting must not imply that every validation query contains 10 or more candidates.

---

# 15. Checkpoint and tuning rule

Hyperparameters are selected using the frozen validation period only.

Apr 22–May 8 labels must not be used for:

- architecture choice;
- hyperparameter selection;
- epoch selection;
- negative-sampling design;
- validation-window selection;
- or training-contract revision.

After model configuration selection:

- each primary model configuration is fitted/frozen according to the frozen training procedure;
- stochastic primary models use the frozen seed set;
- checkpoints are identified and hashed;
- those same fitted checkpoints are reused across all M1 regime × target evaluation conditions.

No separate `is_click` model and `long_view` model may be trained for the primary study.

---

The resulting contract is:

```text

is_click

PAIR COLLAPSE
one user-video pair

exposed user-video pair with no click anywhere

PAIRWISE TRAINING ELIGIBILITY

NEGATIVE SAMPLING

same-user eligible pool
uniform

resampled every epoch

PRIMARY EVALUATION TARGETS
is_click

LONG_VIEW USAGE DURING TRAINING

CHECKPOINT INVARIANT
the same fitted checkpoint for a given model and frozen seed

```

# 17. Decisions explicitly rejected

The following alternatives were considered and rejected for the primary training contract.

| Alternative | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Social actions only | Reject | Too sparse, especially in validation |
| Profile-enter + social composite | Reject | Better coverage but still too sparse for robust primary tuning |
| Every exposure as positive | Reject as primary personalized interaction | Learns exposure-policy structure more than direct preference |
| Require training target to differ from both evaluation targets | Relax | Dataset did not support a strong enough independent behavioral signal |
| Train separate models for `is_click` and `long_view` | Reject | Violates same-checkpoint primary design |
| Arbitrary unseen catalog items as primary negatives | Reject | We have stronger same-user exposed non-click evidence |
| Unique negative required for every positive | Reject | Unnecessarily excludes many users |
| 3-day validation | Reject | Ranking candidate sets too shallow |
| 4-day validation | Reject | Better than 3 days but materially weaker depth than 5 days |
| Choose validation window from model performance | Prohibited | Would introduce avoidable protocol selection bias |

# 18. What remains unfrozen

Freezing the training subsection does **not** mean RankLab M0 is complete.

The overall protocol remains **UNFROZEN** until the remaining primary-study decisions are finalized, including at minimum:

- exact primary M1 logged-ranking unit/candidate construction;
- final common/matched-support policy;
- exact M1 eligibility treatment;
- final primary metric semantics where not already frozen;
- decisive-winner practical threshold;
- uncertainty and bootstrap procedures;
- seed-aggregation and selection implementation;
- direct regime/target contrast implementation;
- final protocol artifact integrity check and hash.

M1 remains blocked until those requirements are satisfied.

---

# 19. What this contract does not claim

The frozen training contract does **not** claim that:

- `is_click` is the universally correct recommendation objective;
- a non-clicked exposed item is a true dislike;
- the standard-policy history is unbiased;
- `long_view` is duration-neutral;
- NDCG@10 is universally optimal;
- training on clicks should optimize long-view performance;
- the frozen validation period is IID with future evaluation logs;
- logged-negative sampling eliminates exposure-policy conditioning;
- BPR or LightGCN is expected to outperform the popularity baseline.

The contract is narrower:

> given the observed KuaiRand-Pure support and RankLab's primary research question, this is the most defensible simple training setup identified before M1 evaluation.

# 21. Final methodological rationale

The most important result of the training-contract work is not that RankLab ended up using clicks.

It is that **the protocol changed when pre-test evidence showed the original design preference was not defensible**.

RankLab did not:

- preserve target independence for aesthetic consistency;
- invent a composite target until one looked convenient;
- use M1 results to choose the training definition;
- select the validation window because a model performed better on it;
- or use aggressive negative sampling merely to maximize user retention.

Instead:

> training-contract decisions were revised using pre-test support, temporal-depth, and feasibility evidence, then frozen before the primary model-comparison study.

That decision process is part of the research record and should remain visible alongside the final frozen configuration.
