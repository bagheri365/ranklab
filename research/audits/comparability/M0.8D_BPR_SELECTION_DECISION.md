**BPR search protocol: FROZEN**

**BPR tuning-seed configuration: SELECTED**

This note records the outcome of the predeclared M0.8c BPR hyperparameter sweep and freezes the BPR configuration selected by that protocol.

The result is based only on the frozen Apr 17–21 standard-policy click-validation objective. No Apr 22–May 8 standard-policy or randomized-exposure evaluation labels were used.

No adaptive grid expansion was permitted after observing results.

## 4. Why this configuration is selected

The winner is selected because it has the highest configuration-level best validation macro NDCG@10 in the predeclared grid.

No post-hoc tolerance, practical-equivalence band, or alternative tie rule was introduced after seeing the sweep.

The nearest observed configurations included:

| Configuration | Best validation NDCG@10 |
|---|---:|
| `d32_lr0.05_reg1e-03` | **0.7659499845152606** |
| `d32_lr0.05_reg1e-04` | 0.7659498003638854 |
| `d32_lr0.05_reg1e-05` | 0.7659498003638854 |
| `d32_lr0.01_reg1e-03` | 0.7659488554401106 |

The gap between the winner and the next-best observed score is approximately:

```text
1.84e-7 NDCG
```

This margin is extremely small.

However, the M0.8c protocol defined selection by strict maximum validation NDCG and reserved special tie-breaking for exact equality only.

Therefore:

> `d32_lr0.05_reg1e-03` is the selected configuration because it is the strict numerical maximum under the frozen rule.

RankLab does not reinterpret this tiny difference as evidence that `regularization=0.001` is meaningfully superior to the nearby alternatives.

## 6. Training-loss interpretation

For the selected configuration, the reported BPR training loss remains numerically close to:

```text
log(2) ≈ 0.693147
```

throughout the full-data run.

For example:

```text
epoch  1: 0.6931521153622098
epoch 10: 0.6931507958085957
epoch 20: 0.6931503835715123
epoch 30: 0.6931501788880200
```

This indicates that under the frozen full-data mini-batch settings, parameter updates are small in aggregate.

This observation must not be hidden.

However, M0.8b separately established on real KuaiRand pairs that the same BPR implementation is capable of learning: under a stronger diagnostic optimization setting on 500 real users, fixed-triplet BPR loss decreased from approximately:

```text
0.6931496065
```

to:

```text
0.6925408840
```

Thus the near-flat full-data sweep loss is not by itself evidence that the implementation is incapable of updating.

It is better interpreted as a property of the selected optimization scale and the shallow logged-candidate validation setup used for this controlled model-selection study.

## 7. Important interpretation of the validation score

The selected validation NDCG@10 is approximately:

```text
0.76595
```

This should not be read as evidence that BPR has achieved an unusually strong unrestricted top-10 recommender result.

The frozen validation candidate construction uses each user's unique logged user-video pairs in Apr 17–21.

Earlier M0 audits established that under the five-day validation window:

```text
median candidates per user: 4
p90 candidates per user:    12
```

Therefore many eligible validation users have fewer than 10 candidates.

NDCG@10 remains well-defined under RankLab's frozen `<K` semantics, but the numeric score reflects a shallow logged-candidate ranking problem rather than full-catalog retrieval.

This is particularly important because the differences among BPR hyperparameter configurations are extremely small.

## 10. No post-hoc sweep expansion

The sweep result does not justify changing the grid.

RankLab will not now:

- search around `regularization=0.001`;
- add values above or below `learning_rate=0.05`;
- add embedding dimensions near 32;
- change the tuning seed because the margin is small;
- add a practical-equivalence tolerance after observing the scores;
- replace strict maximum selection with another rule;
- use Apr 22–May 8 labels to choose among the near-tied configurations.

Doing so would violate the chronology established by M0.8c.

The correct action is to preserve the predeclared winner and document that the validation surface is nearly flat.

---

## 11. Scientific interpretation

The sweep provides two useful findings.

### Finding 1 — BPR configuration selection is numerically executable and reproducible

The runner completed all 27 configurations, selected a winner according to the frozen rule, and reproduced/checksummed the selected artifact.

### Finding 2 — the validation objective is weakly discriminative among nearby BPR settings

Several configurations differ by only tiny fractions of NDCG.

This means the selected hyperparameters should be treated as a controlled protocol choice, not as strong evidence that one specific BPR parameterization is uniquely superior.

That distinction is valuable for RankLab.

The project studies **model-selection stability**. A nearly flat within-model tuning surface is relevant context when later interpreting whether model rankings change across logging regimes and behavioral targets.

## 13. Final rationale

RankLab should freeze this BPR result exactly as observed.

The project gains methodological credibility by recording:

- that the winner is only narrowly ahead;
- that epoch 1 is genuinely the best validation checkpoint;
- that the full-data loss moves only slightly;
- that a separate real-data diagnostic demonstrated optimizer learning capability;
- and that no post-hoc search was opened in response.

The decision is therefore:

> Freeze `d32_lr0.05_reg1e-03` as the BPR configuration selected by the predeclared M0.8c validation protocol, while explicitly recording that the validation surface is nearly flat and that the numerical winning margin is not evidence of practical hyperparameter superiority.
