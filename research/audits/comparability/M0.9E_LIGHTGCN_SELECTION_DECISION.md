# M0.9e — LightGCN Selection Decision

## Status

**LightGCN numerical core: IMPLEMENTED**

**LightGCN real-data pipeline: ENGINEERING-VALID**

**LightGCN optimization-scale audit: COMPLETED**

**LightGCN hyperparameter-search protocol: FROZEN AND EXECUTED**

**LightGCN tuning-seed configuration: SELECTED**

**LightGCN tuning-seed checkpoint: SELECTED**

**Primary multi-seed LightGCN checkpoint set: NOT YET FROZEN**

**Overall M0 protocol: UNFROZEN**

This document freezes the result of the predeclared M0.9d LightGCN hyperparameter search.

It does not reopen the search space.

It records exactly what was selected under the frozen rules, what evidence supports the selection, and what limitations remain.

---

## 1. Frozen search context

The formal M0.9d LightGCN sweep was frozen before execution.

The search contained exactly 27 configurations:

- embedding dimension: `16`, `32`, `64`;
- propagation layers: `1`, `2`, `3`;
- learning rate: `0.5`, `1.0`, `2.0`.

Fixed across the sweep:

- regularization: `1e-4`;
- gradient chunk size: `8192`;
- epochs: `30`;
- initialization std: `0.01`;
- tuning seed: `0`;
- validation metric: macro NDCG@10;
- frozen Apr 9–16 training split;
- frozen Apr 17–21 validation split;
- frozen same-user logged-negative rule;
- graph population restricted to pairwise-eligible positive users.

All 27 configurations completed all 30 epochs.

No early stopping was used.

No adaptive grid expansion was allowed.

No Apr 22–May 8 evaluation labels were used.

No randomized-policy evaluation labels were used.

---

## 2. Frozen selection rule

Within each configuration:

1. choose the strict maximum validation macro NDCG@10;
2. on an exact epoch-level tie, choose the earliest epoch.

Across configurations:

1. choose the strict maximum best validation macro NDCG@10;
2. on an exact configuration-level tie, prefer:
   - fewer propagation layers;
   - lower embedding dimension;
   - lower learning rate;
   - earlier best epoch.

No post-hoc tolerance is introduced.

## 5. Ranking of the strongest configurations

The three best configurations were all 32-dimensional, two-layer LightGCN models:

1. `d32_l2_lr2`
   - best epoch: `28`
   - validation macro NDCG@10: `0.76624771751633`

2. `d32_l2_lr0.5`
   - best epoch: `6`
   - validation macro NDCG@10: `0.7662463559707516`

3. `d32_l2_lr1`
   - best epoch: `1`
   - validation macro NDCG@10: `0.7662442257968052`

The winner's margin over `d32_l2_lr0.5` is approximately:

`1.3615455784e-06`

This is a numerically tiny margin.

The frozen strict-maximum rule still selects `d32_l2_lr2`.

However, RankLab must not interpret this margin as evidence of meaningful practical superiority of learning rate `2.0` over learning rate `0.5`.

The scientifically defensible statement is:

> Under the predeclared selection rule, `d32_l2_lr2` is the numerical tuning-seed winner, but the learning-rate differences inside the strongest `d32_l2` family are extremely small.

No post-hoc tolerance is added to override the frozen rule.

## 6. Strongest architectural signal: propagation depth

At embedding dimension 32, the propagation-depth pattern is much clearer than the learning-rate pattern.

### Two layers

The two-layer family is the strongest region of the search:

- `d32_l2_lr2`: `0.76624771751633`
- `d32_l2_lr0.5`: `0.7662463559707516`
- `d32_l2_lr1`: `0.7662442257968052`

### One layer

The one-layer 32-dimensional family is lower:

- all three one-layer learning-rate variants select approximately `0.7659358348463567`

### Three layers

The three-layer 32-dimensional family is also lower:

- approximately `0.765883` to `0.765891`

Therefore the clearest model-specific result from the LightGCN search is not the exact learning-rate winner.

It is that, under this frozen setup:

> two propagation layers outperform both one and three layers at the strongest 32-dimensional embedding size.

This supports using two layers for the selected LightGCN configuration.

It does not imply that deeper LightGCN architectures are universally worse.

---

## 7. Embedding-dimension pattern

The 32-dimensional family is the strongest capacity region in this sweep.

The 16-dimensional configurations are consistently lower than the best 32-dimensional configurations.

The 64-dimensional configurations are also consistently lower than the best 32-dimensional configurations.

Therefore the frozen search supports selecting embedding dimension 32.

Again, this is a result within the bounded M0.9d grid, not a claim that 32 dimensions is universally optimal.

---

## 8. Selected epoch 28: interpretation

The winning configuration does **not** show a smooth monotonic validation improvement.

Its validation macro NDCG@10 behaves approximately as follows:

- epoch 1: `0.7662442257968052`
- early/middle epochs: small fluctuations, often slightly below epoch 1;
- epoch 23: `0.7662468952814752`
- epoch 25: `0.7662453609493755`
- epoch 27: `0.7662472642892898`
- epoch 28: `0.76624771751633`
- epoch 29: `0.76624771751633`
- epoch 30: `0.7662465614204476`

Epochs 28 and 29 are an **exact validation tie**.

The frozen within-configuration rule therefore selects the earlier epoch:

`28`

This confirms that epoch 28 was not selected because of a unique one-epoch spike.

The winner appears in a small late-epoch high region, with a near-maximum at epoch 27, an exact maximum at epochs 28–29, and a small decline at epoch 30.

At the same time, the differences are tiny.

Therefore RankLab should describe the selected epoch as:

> the earliest epoch achieving the strict numerical maximum under a noisy, near-flat validation trajectory.

It should not describe epoch 28 as evidence that the model requires exactly 28 epochs in any general sense.

---

## 9. Training-loss behavior

The winning LightGCN configuration remains close to `log(2)` in pairwise loss throughout training.

For example:

- epoch 1 loss: `0.6931406396586709`
- epoch 30 loss: `0.693140607676453`

The loss trajectory is non-monotone because the frozen negative sampler resamples same-user logged negatives each epoch.

This near-flat aggregate loss was already anticipated by the M0.9b engineering checks and M0.9c optimization-scale audit.

A separate real-data 500-user diagnostic previously demonstrated that the LightGCN implementation can reduce fixed-triplet pairwise loss under a stronger diagnostic setup.

Therefore the near-flat full-data loss should not be interpreted as proof that the optimizer is incapable of learning.

The more precise interpretation is:

- the implemented LightGCN optimizer can learn;
- aggregate full-data updates under the chosen full-graph formulation are small;
- epoch-to-epoch negative resampling makes the reported loss non-monotone;
- validation ranking changes are correspondingly small.

---

## 10. Relation to the M0.9c optimization-scale audit

The M0.9c audit was not a model-selection sweep.

Its role was to determine a defensible LightGCN learning-rate range after the initial smoke run showed almost no visible movement at learning rate `0.1`.

The formal sweep then used the audit-derived range:

- `0.5`
- `1.0`
- `2.0`

The final winner uses learning rate `2.0`.

However, because the three strongest two-layer learning-rate variants are extremely close, the audit should be understood as having successfully bounded a useful search range rather than having predicted a uniquely superior learning rate.

This distinction should be preserved in the later BPR-vs-LightGCN rationale.

---

## 11. Important BPR-vs-LightGCN contrast

The BPR and LightGCN model-selection paths share the same frozen:

- training dates;
- validation dates;
- click target;
- same-user logged-negative concept;
- tuning seed;
- validation metric;
- strict selection rule philosophy;
- prohibition on evaluation-label access.

But their optimization behavior differs.

### BPR

The selected BPR tuning-seed checkpoint occurred at epoch 1.

The full-data BPR validation trajectory was essentially near-flat, and later epochs did not improve on epoch 1.

### LightGCN

The selected LightGCN tuning-seed checkpoint occurs at epoch 28.

The LightGCN validation trajectory is also near-flat in absolute magnitude, but useful ranking changes emerge late enough that the best region appears near the end of the 30-epoch budget.

This difference is consistent with the earlier observation that the full-graph LightGCN optimizer has a different effective update scale from the mini-batch BPR optimizer.

The later methodology synthesis should retain this contrast rather than forcing the two model-development stories into an artificially identical narrative.

---

## 12. Regularization limitation

Unlike BPR, LightGCN regularization was not independently tuned.

The LightGCN sweep fixed:

`regularization = 1e-4`

The reason was predeclared before execution:

- adding a three-level regularization axis would expand the search from 27 to 81 full-graph configurations;
- the 27-configuration budget was instead allocated to embedding dimension, propagation depth, and the audit-derived learning-rate range.

Therefore RankLab may state:

> LightGCN was tuned over embedding dimension, propagation depth, and learning rate, with regularization fixed at `1e-4`.

RankLab must not state:

> LightGCN regularization was optimized.

This asymmetry with BPR is intentional and documented.

---

## 13. Validation-candidate caveat

The frozen validation unit uses logged candidate sets rather than full-catalog ranking.

From the earlier training-contract audit, validation candidate depth is shallow:

- median candidate count per user: approximately `4`;
- p90 candidate count per user: approximately `12`.

Therefore a macro NDCG@10 around `0.766` must be interpreted as performance on shallow logged candidate sets.

It is not a full-catalog retrieval result.

This caveat applies to both BPR and LightGCN model selection.

## 16. Decision summary

The M0.9d formal search completed all 27 predeclared LightGCN configurations.

Under the frozen strict-maximum rule, the selected tuning-seed configuration is:

`d32_l2_lr2`

with:

- embedding dimension `32`;
- two propagation layers;
- learning rate `2.0`;
- regularization `1e-4`;
- best epoch `28`;
- validation macro NDCG@10 `0.76624771751633`;
- checkpoint SHA256
  `50438670f62a938731eba465fef44302a3e5623572f2e3eb46f0d7824a7cc189`.

The selected checkpoint was physically verified as byte-identical to the winning configuration's best checkpoint.

The strongest scientific signal is the superiority of the two-layer, 32-dimensional region within the bounded search.

The exact learning-rate winner is much less decisive: the strongest learning-rate variants differ only at approximately the sixth decimal place.

The selected epoch is also not interpreted as a sharp optimum: epochs 28 and 29 tie exactly, and the late-epoch validation trajectory is near-flat.

This decision freezes the tuning-seed LightGCN model-selection artifact while leaving primary multi-seed fitting and the remainder of M0 open.
